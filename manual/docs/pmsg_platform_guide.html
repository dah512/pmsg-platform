The PMSG Story

The PMSG platform emerged from a fundamental question: How can we trans-form evidence-based pain management from research findings into reproducible, scalable clinical practice?

After 14 years of clinical practice treating chronic pain patients and 9 years of military service including work on early internet technologies, I recognized a critical gap. We had strong evidence for multidisciplinary pain management programs, but lacked the informatics infrastructure to:


  1. Systematically collect standardized pain assessments (BPI, ODI)

  2. Analyze in real-time patient trajectories and program effectiveness


  3. Generate evidence for continuous quality improvement and research

  4. Scale interventions across diverse healthcare settings

  5. Support clinical decisions with data-driven insights


    The PMSG platform addresses these challenges through a comprehensive, mod-ular system that combines:


    • Clinical rigor: Evidence-based assessments and validated outcome mea-sures

    • Technical excellence: Modern full-stack architecture with robust APIs

    • Analytical depth: Statistical rigor suitable for peer-reviewed publication

    • Practical utility: Real-world workflows for busy clinical settings

    • Research support: Grant applications, IRB submissions, manuscript preparation

        Structure: - Pain Severity: 4 items (worst, least, average, current pain) - Pain Interference: 7 items (general activity, mood, walking, work, relation-ships, sleep, enjoyment of life) - Rating Scale: 0-10 numeric scale (0 = no pain/interference, 10 = worst imaginable)

        Scoring:


        # Pain Severity Score (PSS)

        PSS = mean(worst_pain, least_pain, average_pain, current_pain)


        # Pain Interference Score (PIS)

        PIS = mean(interference_1, interference_2, ..., interference_7)


        # Composite Severity Index

        severity_index = (PSS + PIS) / 2


Interpretation: - Mild Pain: PSS < 4 - Moderate Pain: PSS 4-6 - Severe Pain: PSS > 6


  • Mild Interference: PIS < 3

  • Moderate Interference: PIS 3-6


  • Severe Interference: PIS > 6


Clinically Meaningful Change: - Minimal: 1 point (10% reduction) - Mod-erate: 2 points (20-30% reduction) - Substantial: 3+ points (30%+ reduction)

Psychometric Properties: - Reliability: Cronbach’s = 0.80-0.95 - Valid-ity: Correlates with other pain measures (r = 0.60-0.80) - Responsiveness: Sensitive to treatment effects (SRM = 0.5-0.8) - Languages: Translated and validated in 50+ languages

      1. ‌Other Common Measures

        Pain Catastrophizing Scale (PCS): - 13 items assessing rumination, mag-nification, helplessness - Score 0-52 (>30 indicates clinically significant catas-trophizing) - Strong predictor of treatment outcome

        Tampa Scale for Kinesiophobia (TSK): - 17 items assessing fear of move-ment/reinjury - Score 17-68 (>37 indicates high fear-avoidance) - Targets for behavioral interventions

        Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC): - Single item: “Overall, how much has your pain changed?” - 7-point scale (very much worse to very much improved) - Anchors other outcome measures

        SF-36 / SF-12 (Short Form Health Survey): - Generic health-related quality of life - 8 domains: physical functioning, role limitations, pain, gen-eral health, vitality, social functioning, emotional role, mental health - Enables comparison across conditions

        PROMIS (Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System): - Modern item response theory-based measures - Computer adaptive testing (CAT) - Multiple domains: pain intensity, pain interference, physical function, depression, anxiety - Growing adoption in clinical research


    1. ‌Program Models and Formats

      MPPs vary in intensity, duration, and delivery format.


      1. ‌Intensive Inpatient Programs

        Characteristics: - 3-4 weeks residential - 40-50 hours/week treatment - Highly structured schedule - Immersive environment

        Advantages: - Rapid improvement - Complete focus on recovery - Removal from pain-maintaining environment - Intensive monitoring

        Disadvantages: - High cost ($15,000-30,000) - Work/family disruption - Lim-ited geographic availability - Transition challenges to home environment

        Typical Candidates: - Severe, chronic, treatment-refractory pain - High dis-ability levels (ODI > 60%) - Significant psychological comorbidity - Failed out-patient treatment


      2. ‌Intensive Outpatient Programs

        Characteristics: - 2-4 weeks duration - 20-30 hours/week - Daily attendance

        • Return home evenings

          Advantages: - Lower cost than inpatient - Maintain family connections - Prac-tice skills in real environment - Easier access

          Disadvantages: - Transportation requirements - Ongoing exposure to stressors

        • Less intensive monitoring - Requires stable home situation

        Typical Candidates: - Moderate-severe pain - Moderate disability (ODI 40-60%) - Stable living situation - Reliable transportation


      3. ‌Standard Outpatient Programs

        Characteristics: - 6-12 weeks duration - 6-12 hours/week - 2-3 sessions per week - Integrated with normal activities

        Advantages: - Most affordable - Flexible scheduling - Gradual skill building - Real-world application

        Disadvantages: - Slower progress - Less intensive support - Competing de-mands - Requires strong motivation

        Typical Candidates: - Mild-moderate pain - Lower disability (ODI < 40%) - Working individuals - Strong family support
